# Larga: Research Backing [AI: REFERENCE]

Evidence that the problems Larga solves are real. Scope: problems found across the Philippines; the product starts in **Metro Naga**, then expands to the rest of Bicol.

**Positioning:** Larga is a driver and passenger platform for **public transport** (jeepneys, tricycles, buses), not for private ride-hailing. Commuters keep paying the regulated public fare instead of private-car prices.

**How to read the strength column**
- **Strong:** peer-reviewed study or official government source
- **Medium:** reputable news report, or a survey with a stated method
- **Weak:** small or advocacy survey, blog, or marketing page. Use only as color, never as the headline stat.

Everything was checked online on Oct 26, 2026. Anything not confirmed is marked **verify**.

---

## Headline lines for the pitch

1. **About 1.8 million devotees** joined the 2025 Peñafrancia fluvial procession in Naga. Many of them are visitors who have to find a ride in a city they don't know. (Naga City Government)
2. **We found no commute app that covers Naga.** Sakay.ph, the best-known PH commute app, covers Metro Manila only, and Naga's 2025 route plan is published only as council documents and PDFs.
3. **Naga's own fare ordinance makes the ₱15 tricycle fare conditional on monitoring violations**, and it requires the fare matrix to be posted on every tricycle. The city already treats overcharging as a problem. (City Ord. 2024-002)
4. **3 in 5 women** have experienced sexual harassment while commuting in Metro Manila. (UN Women and Quezon City baseline study, 2016)
5. **Real-time arrival info cut riders' actual waits by almost 2 minutes and their perceived waits by about 13%.** (Watkins et al., *Transportation Research Part A*, 2011)
6. **The national PUV Modernization Program expects coops to use a fleet management system**, and one Pasig jeepney coop paid **₱45,000 a month** (₱1,500 per unit, 30 units) for one. (PUVMP; CURODA-TSC case, written 2021)

## Who benefits (the commuting ecosystem)

| Group | What they get | Backed by |
|---|---|---|
| **Commuters** (students, newcomers, Peñafrancia visitors, regular riders) | Know the terminal, the vehicle and the **official fare, including the discount** before riding; spend less time waiting without knowing; a record of the vehicle they rode | Pain points 1 to 4 |
| **Drivers** | Riders find the vehicle that's loading, so it fills faster; a **coop-verified identity** separates them from colorum vehicles; a visible official fare means fewer arguments; payment by the coop's QR Ph code or in cash | Colorum crackdowns; fare-dispute news; QR Ph adoption (below) |
| **Coops and TODAs** | Passenger counts and trip records per vehicle, for dispatch and the fleet management system PUVMP expects, at a fraction of the cost of enterprise systems | PUVMP; ₱45k/month Pasig case; FILIPAY |
| **LGU** (Naga PSO, City Council) | Fare compliance it can actually see, which **Ord. 2024-002 already requires**; a digital copy of the fare matrix the city must already post on every tricycle; a public map of the rationalized routes; easier transport for Peñafrancia visitors | Ord. 2024-002; Res. 204 s.2025; 1.8M visitors; new HUC status |

## Business angle

- **Who pays:** coops, TODAs or the LGU pay a small monthly fee per terminal. Commuters use it for free.
- **Willingness to pay already exists:** a Pasig coop paid about ₱45,000 a month for dispatch software, Mandaue City signed for a tracking map, and CDO coops buy commercial fleet systems.
- **Low cost to run:** it uses phones drivers already have and needs no GPS hardware. Payments go through QR Ph, which the market already uses: **2.47 billion QR Ph transactions in 2025**, more than cards for the first time, and digital payments reached **64.7% of retail payments**. ([Manila Bulletin, Aug 2026](https://mb.com.ph/2026/08/17/digital-payments-hit-647-of-philippine-retail-transactions-nearing-bsps-70-target), [Context.ph](https://context.ph/2026/08/18/digital-payments-hit-target-as-filipinos-shift-from-cash/))
- **Users can reach it:** 97.5 million Filipinos (83.8%) were online in early 2025. ([DataReportal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2025-philippines))
- **Room to grow:** Naga first, then the rest of Bicol. Every LGU must file a route plan (LPTRP) before PUV franchises open, so every city has routes that need mapping.

## Gaps: what we could not find (don't claim these)

- **No data on overcharging in Naga itself.** We have Naga's ordinance and news from other cities, but no Naga complaint counts.
- **No national survey on commuter safety.** The best data is the 2016 Quezon City study.
- **No study measuring how long PH jeeps wait at terminals until they fill up.**
- **Whether Grab operates in Naga today is unconfirmed** (a 2020 report lists GrabTaxi in Naga).

**Best fix: a quick field survey.** Ask 15 to 20 commuters at a Naga terminal (SM, Centro or a jeepney terminal) four questions:
1. Have you ever been unsure which jeep or tricycle to take, or where its terminal is?
2. Have you ever been charged more than the posted fare, or been refused your discount?
3. How long do you usually wait for the vehicle to leave, and do you know when it will?
4. Would you use a free app that shows the fare and whether a vehicle is loading?

Even 15 answers from Naga riders count for more with the judges than any national statistic.
